Venue Assessment and Spatial Planning
Every successful event setup begins with a proper venue assessment. Before drawing a layout or ordering equipment, event professionals must understand the space dimensions, environmental conditions, and functional requirements of the venue. This topic trains learners to evaluate indoor and outdoor spaces, record accurate measurements, and create effective spatial plans that support safety, movement, and comfort.

Fig 28: Venue Assessment 
Learning Objectives
 By the end of this topic, students should be able to:
Conduct a thorough venue site inspection and collect essential spatial and logistical data.
Measure, record, and sketch the usable area of a venue accurately.
Identify potential challenges and opportunities that may affect event setup, flow, and safety.
Apply principles of spatial planning to optimize guest movement, staff efficiency, and overall experience.
Consider environmental, logistical, and regulatory factors when assessing event spaces.
1. Understanding Venue Assessment
Venue assessment is a critical step in planning any event, from small gatherings to large conferences. Conducting an onsite evaluation allows event professionals to anticipate challenges, ensure compliance with regulations, and identify ways to enhance guest experience.
Purpose of Site Visits in Event Planning
Verify details provided in venue brochures or online listings.
Confirm the capacity and suitability for intended activities.
Understand the natural lighting, ventilation, and acoustics.
Evaluate accessibility for guests, suppliers, and emergency services.


Checklist for Site Inspection
A detailed checklist ensures nothing is overlooked:
Venue location and accessibility – Consider proximity to transport hubs, parking, and traffic conditions.
Entry and exit points – Identify primary and emergency exits for safety compliance.
Lighting and ventilation – Assess natural light, artificial lighting, and airflow to ensure comfort.
Electrical and water access – Determine locations of outlets, power capacity, and plumbing facilities.
Washrooms, storage, and catering areas – Confirm adequacy for expected guest numbers.


Fig 29: Site inspection checklist  
Security and parking space – Evaluate on-site or nearby security measures and parking availability.


Recording observations and measurements – Maintain detailed notes and sketches to inform layout and logistics.


Measuring the Space
Accurate measurements are essential for creating functional layouts and preventing last-minute adjustments.
Techniques for Measuring Dimensions
Use measuring tapes or laser distance tools to obtain precise length, width, and height measurements.


Note both usable and restricted areas, such as columns, walls, uneven flooring, or steps.


Fig 30: Site measurement from blueprints, if available  

Sketch a simple, scaled site plan that includes all critical features.
Record locations of electrical outlets, water taps, emergency exits, and other infrastructure.


Identifying Usable vs. Restricted Areas
Mark columns, staircases, ramps, and other obstacles.
Assess ceiling height for stage setups, lighting rigs, and decorations.
Evaluate flooring type to ensure stability for seating, dance floors, and equipment.


Principles of Spatial Planning
Proper spatial planning ensures smooth guest movement, staff efficiency, and safety compliance.
Importance of Flow
Design pathways that minimize congestion for guests, suppliers, and staff.
Separate areas for different activities to reduce interference (e.g., catering near kitchen, away from stage).


Balancing Functional Zones
Plan for seating arrangements, stage setup, catering stations, entertainment areas, and washrooms.
Ensure each zone is proportionate to expected use and capacity.


Safety and Compliance
Maintain clear emergency paths.
Ensure adequate spacing between tables, chairs, and equipment.
Anchor tents, decorations, and stages securely to prevent hazards.


Environmental Considerations
Outdoor events: account for weather conditions, wind direction, ground drainage, and sunlight.
Indoor events: check HVAC systems, artificial lighting, and acoustics.
Accessibility: ensure ramps, lifts, and pathways meet regulatory standards.


Practical Session (4 Hours)


Activity 1: Site Visit Simulation
- Students visit an event venue, open space, or external venue nearby.
- Measure and sketch the area, including entry points, fixed structures, and usable zones.
- Record observations about lighting, ventilation, access points, and potential hazards.

Convert measurements into a simple, scaled floor plan.
Label all zones, including entrance, stage, catering, washrooms, exit, storage, and technical areas.

Activity 3: Flow Design Exercise
Using the same plan, map out paths for guests, staff, and suppliers.
Highlight areas that could cause congestion or safety concerns.
Suggest adjustments to improve movement, such as rearranging seating or relocating equipment.


Activity 4: Environmental Impact Planning
Identify outdoor-specific considerations, such as shade, wind, rain, or drainage issues.
Plan contingency strategies like tents, fans, or floor coverings.
Assess indoor ventilation and temperature control for guest comfort.



Assessment
Observation of students during the site visit for engagement, accuracy, and attention to detail.
Submission of a detailed venue sketch, including all zones, measurements, and flow paths.
Class presentations on challenges identified and proposed solutions to optimize the space.
Peer review of venue plans to encourage collaborative problem-solving.

Equipment Identification and Selection
Lesson Overview
Event success heavily depends on the right choice and handling of equipment — from tents, stages, and furniture to lighting and sound systems. This topic introduces learners to the wide range of equipment used in event production, how to select the right types based on event scale and venue, and how to ensure safe handling, installation, and maintenance.

Fig 32: Tents, furniture, and lights:  Examples of event equipment   
Learning Objectives
 By the end of this topic, students should be able to:
Identify the broad range of equipment used in various types of events, understanding each item’s purpose and operational requirements.
Match appropriate equipment to different event types, audience sizes, and themes, ensuring functional and aesthetic alignment.
Accurately estimate quantities needed for diverse setups, including seating, lighting, décor, and technical systems.
Demonstrate correct and safe handling, installation, and dismantling procedures for all categories of event equipment.
Prepare comprehensive event equipment checklists and inventory forms to ensure organized planning, accountability, and efficiency.
Assess the suitability of equipment considering environmental conditions, safety standards, and budget constraints.
Integrate equipment planning with spatial layout and guest experience objectives.
Identify potential challenges during sourcing, setup, or operation and propose practical solutions.



1. Categories of Event Equipment
Event equipment is grouped based on function, utility, and purpose. Understanding these categories helps in planning, sourcing, and setup efficiently.
Shelter & Structure Equipment
Tents, marquees, canopies, and pavilions for outdoor events.
Stages, platforms, and truss frames for performance or presentation areas.
Modular structures for temporary exhibition booths, VIP lounges, or registration desks.
Considerations: wind resistance, anchoring, fire retardancy, and weight load capacity.


Furniture & Fittings
Tables, chairs, sofas, podiums, and partitions for seating arrangements and guest comfort.
Bar counters, buffet tables, and display stands to facilitate service and visual presentation.
Adjustable or foldable furniture for space optimization.
Considerations: ergonomics, style alignment with theme, material durability, and ease of transport.


Décor & Lighting
Drapes, table centerpieces, floral arrangements, and thematic props.
Chandeliers, LED uplights, fairy lights, spotlights, and projection screens.
Ambient and decorative lighting to enhance mood and highlight key areas.
Considerations: power consumption, color temperature, placement for visual impact, and safety of hanging fixtures.


Sound & Audio Equipment
Microphones (wired, wireless, lapel), speakers, mixers, amplifiers, and PA systems.
Audio distribution and zone coverage for large venues.
Specialized equipment for live performances, speeches, or background music.
Considerations: feedback management, cable routing, volume levels, and technical support requirements.


Power & Utility Equipment
Generators, extension cords, cables, distribution boards, and surge protectors.
UPS systems for critical electronics.
Lighting and AV backup systems.
Considerations: fuel supply, load capacity, safety compliance, and redundancy planning.
Catering & Service Equipment
Chafing dishes, buffet tables, food warmers, glassware, crockery, and cutlery.
Serving stations, beverage dispensers, and bar setups.
Considerations: hygiene, placement for efficient service flow, and integration with event layout.
Miscellaneous Equipment
Signage, crowd barriers, fencing, bins, mobile toilets, red carpets, and wayfinding systems.
Considerations: durability, visibility, and alignment with event branding.

2. Equipment Selection Criteria
Selecting the right equipment is critical to event success. The decision should factor in multiple variables:
Type of Event
Corporate conferences require AV, podiums, and seating.
Weddings focus on décor, furniture, lighting, and floral arrangements.
Concerts demand stages, sound systems, lighting rigs, and crowd barriers.
Fig 33: Event AV systems    
Venue Conditions
Indoor versus outdoor settings affect structure choices, weather protection, and technical installation.
Assess ground surfaces, accessibility, ventilation, and natural lighting.
Evaluate space restrictions for safety compliance and operational flow.


Guest Capacity
Ensure seating, tables, and service areas align with expected attendee numbers.
Allow sufficient spacing for comfort, emergency access, and staff operations.


Theme & Décor
Match furniture, lighting, and décor to color palettes, brand requirements, or thematic concepts.
Consider style consistency between zones and visual focal points.


Budget
Compare hire versus ownership costs for high-value or rarely used equipment.
Optimize rental duration to avoid unnecessary expenses.
Factor in labor and transport costs in the overall budget.


Safety Standards
Ensure all equipment is certified, stable, and installed according to manufacturer guidelines.
Plan for emergency access, evacuation routes, and secure anchoring of structures.




3. Equipment Sourcing
Efficient sourcing reduces risk and ensures timely availability:
Working with Rental Companies and Suppliers
Build relationships with reliable suppliers.
Understand terms, delivery schedules, and setup support offered.


Quality and Availability Checks
Inspect equipment before hire or purchase for defects or wear.
Schedule procurement in advance, especially during peak seasons or major events.


Quotation and Deposit Systems
Compare quotes from multiple vendors.
Understand deposit, cancellation, and replacement policies.


Equipment Tracking and Accountability
Use tracking sheets or digital inventory systems to monitor equipment allocation, condition, and return.
Assign responsibilities for setup, operation, and dismantling to specific team members.

Group presentation of equipment list, quantity estimations, and placement strategy.

Layout Design and Drafting
Lesson Overview
This topic introduces students to the art and technique of designing event layouts. Learners will practice converting an event brief into a professional layout drawing, either manually or using simple digital tools. They will apply spatial design principles to organize functional zones such as seating, stage, catering, and walkways. The emphasis is on accuracy, balance, and functionality in event layout drafting.

Fig 34: Event Layout and Design  

Learning Objectives
By the end of this topic, students should be able to:
Interpret complex event briefs, identifying client requirements, objectives, and spatial constraints.
Translate written and verbal instructions into detailed, accurate layout plans suitable for both small and large-scale events.
Sketch scaled event layouts demonstrating correct placement of furniture, equipment, and functional zones.
Apply fundamental design principles, including balance, proportion, flow, and focal points, to enhance aesthetic appeal and functionality.
Utilize both manual drafting and digital tools to create professional event layouts.
Label, annotate, and color-code layouts for clarity, communication, and approval processes.
Present layout concepts clearly to clients, supervisors, or team members, explaining design choices and operational logic.
Identify potential logistical or safety issues within a layout and propose effective solutions.
Adapt layouts to different event types, themes, and spatial restrictions.
Critically evaluate peer designs and provide constructive feedback.




1. Understanding Layout Design
Event layout design is a cornerstone of effective event management. A well-planned layout ensures smooth flow, maximizes space utility, and enhances the guest experience.
Importance of Layout Visualization
Provides a clear representation of spatial organization before execution.
Helps identify potential bottlenecks or conflicts in movement, equipment placement, or service areas.
Supports communication between clients, vendors, and internal teams.


Translating Client Requirements
Review event briefs carefully: note objectives, expected number of guests, theme, and special requests.
Consider functional needs: seating, dining, entertainment, catering, registration, and emergency access.
Incorporate visual and branding elements into the layout.


Key Design Principles
Balance: Ensure distribution of furniture, décor, and equipment evenly across the space for visual harmony and functional efficiency.
Flow: Plan clear pathways for guests, staff, and suppliers; minimize congestion points and ensure safety compliance.
Proportion: Align furniture and equipment sizes with available space to avoid overcrowding or underutilization.
Focal Point: Highlight key areas such as stages, ceremonial zones, or main displays to draw attention and guide guest focus.
Hierarchy: Prioritize zones based on function, importance, and visibility.
Flexibility: Design layouts that can accommodate last-minute changes or variations in guest numbers.




2. Drafting Techniques
Manual Drawing
Use graph paper, rulers, and scale conversion (e.g., 1cm = 1m or 1:50) for accurate representation.
Draw boundaries, walls, entrances/exits, and fixed structures first.
Add furniture, equipment, service stations, and décor sequentially.
Annotate clearly with dimensions, labels, and symbols.

Fig 35: Example of an event layout drawing 
Digital Tools
Introduce beginner-friendly software like Canva, SketchUp, SmartDraw, or Floorplanner.
Teach basic functions: drawing walls, placing objects, layering elements, and adding annotations.
Emphasize exporting layouts for presentation or sharing with clients.


Labeling & Notation
Mark all functional zones, such as seating types, stage, catering stations, washrooms, storage, and security points.
Include emergency exits, power outlets, lighting positions, and signage locations.
Use clear symbols or legends for easy interpretation by other team members.


Color Coding & Aesthetics
Apply color or shading to differentiate zones: guest areas, service zones, staff pathways.
Maintain visual clarity and consistency to facilitate comprehension.
Incorporate theme-related colors or branding elements for realistic simulation.


3. Elements to Include in a Layout Draft
Stage or Performance Area: Ensure visibility and access from all seating zones.
Seating Arrangement: Adapt to event type (banquet, classroom, U-shape, theatre, cocktail, etc.).
Entrance and Exit Paths: Ensure clear and safe access, including emergency exits.
Catering/Service Stations: Strategically place buffets, bars, and food/water stations to optimize flow.
Registration/Help Desk: Positioned near main entrance for easy access.
Washrooms, Storage, and Security Points: Include support facilities while maintaining discreet placement.
Electrical and Lighting Positions: Plan for AV setup, power supply, and safety compliance.
Additional Zones: VIP areas, photo booths, decorative focal points, and interactive stations.



Practical Activities (10 Hours)
Activity 1 – Drafting from a Brief (3 Hours)
Students receive a sample event brief, including client requirements, guest numbers, and theme.
Measure the provided space (or use a standard mock layout).
Sketch a scaled layout showing all key zones, furniture, and equipment placement.
Annotate with dimensions, pathways, and notes on functional considerations.


Activity 2 – Digital Layout Practice (3 Hours)
Redraw manual sketches using digital design tools.
Focus on precision, clarity, and visual appeal.
Add labels, color coding, and legends for zones, equipment, and safety features.


Activity 3 – Presentation and Feedback (2 Hours)

Evaluate each layout based on clarity, proportion, and compliance with brief requirements.

Modify the existing design to suit a different event type (e.g., converting a wedding layout into a corporate conference setup).
Emphasize adaptability, space optimization, and problem-solving skills.
Present redesigned layout and discuss adjustments made for function, aesthetics, and safety.


Activity 5 – Scenario-Based Adjustments (Optional Extension)
Introduce unexpected variables: additional guests, equipment failure, or weather constraints.
Students update their layout to resolve issues while maintaining design principles.
Discuss decision-making and contingency planning.



Assessment
Accuracy, scale, and completeness of drawings (manual and digital).
Effective application of design principles: balance, flow, proportion, and focal points.
Creativity, clarity, and professionalism in layout presentation.
Peer and instructor evaluations on adaptability, problem-solving, and communication.
Optional reflective journal on design challenges and learning outcomes.



Materials Required
Graph papers, pencils, rulers, erasers, and colored markers.
Sample event briefs with spatial measurements.
Laptops or tablets with beginner-friendly design software.
Reference photos or case studies of professional event setups.
Optional: projector or screen for presenting digital layouts.

Event Setup Procedures
Lesson Overview
This topic focuses on the actual setup of event layouts and equipment, applying all the skills learned in previous topics — layout design, equipment selection, and space planning. Learners will take part in real or simulated event setups, from delivery and arrangement to testing and inspection, under instructor supervision.
It emphasizes teamwork, timing, safety, and coordination among setup crews to ensure smooth execution.

Fig 36: A well setup intimate event 

Learning Objectives
By the end of this topic, learners should be able to:
Organize and manage all event equipment and materials to ensure a smooth and efficient setup and teardown process.
Assemble event layouts accurately according to approved floor plans and layout designs.
Apply proper lifting techniques, ergonomics, and safety procedures when handling heavy or awkward items.
Coordinate workflow among team members to optimize productivity and prevent delays.
Troubleshoot common setup challenges, including spatial constraints, equipment malfunction, electrical issues, and environmental factors such as weather.
Communicate effectively with supervisors, clients, and peers during setup and teardown operations.
Prioritize tasks, estimate setup duration, and sequence activities to maximize efficiency.
Maintain high standards of cleanliness, aesthetics, and functional readiness throughout the event space.


1. Pre-Setup Preparation
 Pre-setup preparation is critical to ensure that the event runs smoothly, safely, and on schedule.
Review Event Layout Plan and Setup Checklist
Confirm all dimensions, equipment requirements, and placement points.


Check for any last-minute changes from the client or supervisor.


Ensure contingency plans are noted for possible issues (weather, additional guests, technical failures).


Assign Roles and Responsibilities
Divide teams based on expertise: tents, décor, lighting, sound, catering, registration, and logistics.


Assign team leaders to monitor progress and report issues.


Brief all team members on safety protocols, communication channels, and expected timelines.


Loading and Transport Logistics
Pack and label all equipment systematically to facilitate quick offloading.
Plan transport routes to minimize damage and ensure timely delivery.
Confirm all tools, fasteners, and accessories are included before leaving storage.



Site Readiness Inspection
Check ground leveling and stability for tents, stages, and heavy equipment.
Verify access to electricity, water, and emergency points.
Identify and mark restricted zones, hazards, and areas requiring additional support.

2. Setup Procedures
 Following a structured step-by-step approach reduces errors and enhances safety.
Tent Assembly and Anchoring
Inspect tent components for defects.
Assemble the frame according to manufacturer instructions.
Use proper anchoring techniques and weights to prevent collapse or wind damage.


Stage and Platform Setup
Ensure platforms are level and securely connected.
Test weight-bearing capacity before placing equipment or performers.
Coordinate with lighting and sound teams to integrate cabling and rigging.




Fig 36: Event Setup Process Example 

Table and Chair Arrangement
Arrange seating according to layout type (banquet, theatre, classroom, cocktail).
Maintain sufficient spacing for guest comfort, emergency access, and service efficiency.
Check stability and alignment.




Lighting and Sound Installation
Position lights, LED setups, chandeliers, and decorative lighting according to plan.
Install microphones, speakers, mixers, and amplifiers with proper cable management.
Test all equipment for functionality and safety compliance.


Décor and Branding Elements
Place banners, signage, floral arrangements, and thematic props carefully.
Ensure decorations do not obstruct pathways, exits, or safety equipment.
Finalize aesthetic touches such as table settings and centerpieces.


Catering Stations and Service Areas
Set up food and beverage stations with consideration for flow and guest access.
Ensure compliance with hygiene and safety standards.
Coordinate with kitchen or catering staff for timely delivery and replenishment.
Coordination and Communication
Use radios, phones, or visual signals to maintain coordination between teams.
Team leaders provide real-time updates to ensure smooth workflow.
Resolve minor issues quickly without disrupting overall setup sequence.
Time Management
Sequence tasks efficiently: tents and structures first, followed by staging, seating, technical setup, and décor.
Allocate estimated hours per section, allowing buffer time for troubleshooting.
Monitor progress against schedule to ensure readiness before the event.


3. Post-Setup Checks
After setup, verification ensures everything functions as intended and meets client expectations.
Test all electrical systems, including sound, lighting, and power distribution.
Walk through the event layout to verify alignment with the approved plan.
Adjust table arrangements, décor, and equipment placement as needed.
Perform cleaning and final aesthetic touches to enhance visual appeal.
Conduct a walkthrough with supervisor or client for feedback and approval.
Document any discrepancies, damage, or safety concerns for reporting.


4. Teardown and Storage
 Proper dismantling protects equipment and ensures readiness for future events.
Disassemble items systematically, starting with delicate or hazardous components.
Clean, count, and pack all equipment carefully to prevent damage.
Check against inventory lists to ensure nothing is missing.
Transport items back to storage or return to suppliers.
Update maintenance notes and checklists for repairs, replacements, or replenishment.
Reflect on setup and teardown processes to identify areas for improvement.

Principles of Design in Event Layout
Lesson Overview
Event layouts are not just about functionality — they are also about visual harmony, flow, and aesthetics. A well-designed event space creates mood, directs attention, and enhances guest experience.
This topic introduces students to the principles of design and composition used in event layout and décor. Learners will understand how proportion, emphasis, movement, repetition, variety, and unity combine to create a cohesive and attractive setup.
These design principles are essential for event professionals to transform ordinary spaces into well-organized and captivating environments that reflect a client’s vision and brand.

Learning Objectives
By the end of this topic, learners should be able to:
Define and explain the six key principles of design in event layout.
Apply design principles to create balanced and functional event spaces.
Evaluate event setups for harmony, visual appeal, and flow.
Demonstrate creativity and aesthetic judgment during layout exercises.

1. Proportion
Refers to the size relationship between elements in a space.
Ensures furniture, stage, décor, and lighting complement each other.
Avoids overpowering or underwhelming visual balance.
When all parts of a design—such as the size, height, and spacing of elements—relate harmoniously to each other and to the room’s dimensions. In this setup, the medium-height tables and gold Chiavari chairs are well-proportioned to the high wooden ceiling and spacious hall. 
Fig 37: Executing flawless proportions in event layouts 
The length of the rectangular tables balances the width of the room, while the uniform table settings and greenery runners ensure that the décor doesn’t feel either too heavy or too sparse. The visual rhythm between the round and long tables also maintains proportion, creating a cohesive and comfortable environment without overwhelming the rustic interior.


2. Emphasis
Emphasis refers to the focal point in a design — the element that immediately attracts attention and creates visual interest.
In this setup, the large white globe lanterns suspended from the high, arched wooden ceiling command instant attention. Their bright color and uniform repetition contrast beautifully with the warm tones of the timber interior, naturally guiding the viewer’s eye upward and along the length of the room. The glowing lanterns not only add drama but also create vertical movement, emphasizing the height and structure of the barn while anchoring the entire décor concept.
Fig 38: the event venue shows the emphasis of the focal point at the front  
This strong visual emphasis balances the otherwise rustic setting, making the lighting design the centerpiece that ties the theme together.
3. Movement
Movement refers to the visual flow or the path your eye naturally follows as it travels around a design or space.
In this elegant tented wedding setup, the floral arrangements and ceiling draping create graceful movement throughout the room. The clusters of flowers placed atop each column and the large floral installation at the peak of the ceiling draw the viewer’s eye upward, then outward along the soft curves of the draped fabric. The gentle lines of twinkle lights further enhance this motion, leading the eye from the center chandelier toward the edges of the tent.
This thoughtful use of line, height, and repetition allows guests to visually explore the entire space, creating a feeling of continuity and flow that keeps the décor dynamic and engaging.






Fig 39: A well designed event with movement as a key feature. 
4. Repetition
Repetition involves the consistent use of elements—such as shapes, colors, textures, or materials—to establish rhythm and a sense of unity throughout the design.
In this grand indoor reception, repetition is clearly seen in the arrangement of round tables, each draped in identical neutral linens and centered with matching floral arrangements. The uniform gold Chiavari chairs and soft amber lighting reinforce a cohesive atmosphere. The repetition of arched doorways wrapped in twinkling string lights and candle-style chandeliers creates rhythm that leads the eye evenly across the entire room.
Fig 40. Example of repetition in event layout design 
This consistent use of form and color ties the vast space together, giving it a polished, professional finish while maintaining warmth and harmony.


5. Variety
Variety adds visual interest and contrast by introducing different shapes, colors, textures, or materials while still maintaining a unified style. It keeps the design dynamic and engaging rather than repetitive or flat.
In this elegant ballroom setting, variety is expressed through the mix of floral heights and table centerpieces — tall arrangements of calla lilies complement the lower floral clusters, creating layers of visual appeal. The rich golden chairs, textured linens, and ambient wall lighting blend different materials and tones that enhance the luxurious feel of the room.

This topic equips learners with essential knowledge and hands-on skills for managing electrical systems, lighting installations, and general on-site safety. Students will learn to identify potential hazards, use proper protective equipment, and comply with occupational health and safety guidelines applicable to event environments.

Identify electrical and physical hazards in event setups.
Apply safe handling practices for lighting, sound, and power equipment.
Demonstrate proper cable management and grounding.
Use safety gear and tools correctly.
Respond appropriately to emergencies and minor incidents.

1. Introduction to Event Safety
Importance of safety compliance in event operations.
Legal and institutional safety requirements.
The role of supervisors and safety marshals.

2. Electrical Safety
Understanding power distribution: main supply, generator, control board, and backup.

Evacuation procedures and first aid protocols.
Reporting incidents to supervisors or authorities.

Equipment Maintenance and Dismantling
Lesson Overview
After every event, proper equipment maintenance and dismantling are critical to protect company assets, ensure safety, and prepare for future use. This topic trains learners in post-event processes — from careful teardown, cleaning, and inspection, to inventory documentation and storage organization.
Students will also learn how to identify damaged equipment and follow reporting or repair procedures.

Learning Objectives
By the end of this topic, students should be able to:
Apply safe dismantling procedures for tents, stages, lighting, and décor.
Clean and maintain event equipment correctly.
Identify faulty or damaged items and report them for repair.
Record and update inventory after events.
Organize and store equipment systematically for easy retrieval.

1. Dismantling Procedures
Follow the reverse order of setup (e.g., start with lighting and décor before tents and stages).
Switch off and disconnect all electrical sources.
Use proper lifting methods to avoid injury.
Stack and group similar equipment for easy counting.
Ensure safety during teardown (no dismantling while guests or vendors are still on site).

2. Equipment Cleaning and Maintenance
Cleaning materials: soft cloths, brushes, mild detergents.
Maintenance routines:
Wipe and air-dry chairs, tables, and tents.
Inspect electrical cords for burns or frays.
Test sound systems and lighting for faults.
Oil hinges and joints on mechanical parts (stages, tent frames).
Labeling repaired or faulty items for follow-up.

3. Storage and Inventory Management
Organizing storage by category and frequency of use (e.g., tents in one area, lights in another).
Using pallets or racks to prevent floor damage.
Tagging equipment with barcodes or labels.
Recording in inventory forms or digital logs:
Equipment name
Quantity returned
Condition (good, damaged, missing)
Maintenance notes

Practical Activities (6 Hours)
Activity 1 – Supervised Teardown (2 hrs)
Students dismantle a mock event setup, following safety and sequence guidelines.
Activity 2 – Cleaning and Inspection (2 hrs)
Teams clean tables, chairs, tents, and cables, noting any wear or faults.
Activity 3 – Inventory Update (2 hrs)
Students fill maintenance forms, tag faulty items, and organize storage space neatly.

Assessment
Observation during dismantling and cleaning.
Completion and accuracy of maintenance checklists.
Team coordination and safe working practices.
Submission of an equipment report and photo log (optional).

Materials Required
Cleaning tools and detergents
Repair kits (tape, pliers, oil, replacement bulbs)
Storage shelves or racks
Maintenance and inventory forms
PPE (gloves, masks, reflective vests)
